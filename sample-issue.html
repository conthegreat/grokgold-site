The week in one line

Gold had its worst week since March, falling roughly 9–10% to a 14-month low near $3,500/oz, as markets priced in a December Fed rate hike, the dollar firmed and an AI-led equity sell-off triggered forced selling. [1][2]

What happened

  • Price: Spot gold fell about 3% on Friday to around $3,514/oz, after touching $3,502.49, its lowest since September 2025. US December gold futures settled 4.6% lower at $3,515.40. That's down about 9.4% on the week. [2]
  • From record to rout: Gold set an all-time high of $5,589.38 on 28 January this year. On Wednesday it fell below $3,800 for the first time since September 2025, roughly a third below that peak. [3]
  • The Fed: Markets now price about a 96% chance of a 25bp increase at the 8–9 December FOMC, per CME FedWatch as reported by Reuters. Philadelphia Fed President Anna Paulson flagged inflation risks, and Governor Christopher Waller said a 50bp move might be needed to "shock people". [1][3]
  • Dollar and yields: The dollar climbed to a 19-month high, and higher Treasury yields raised the opportunity cost of holding a metal that pays no income. [3][4]
  • Cross-asset spill-over: High Ridge Futures' David Meger linked part of the selling to forced liquidations to cover losses in the AI-related equity sell-off, which deepened after Anthropic's IPO failed to lift the sector. [1]
  • Silver hit harder: Silver is down around 41% over six months versus about 19% for gold, trading near $41. [4]

The other side of the ledger

Not every data point is negative. Central banks recorded their highest monthly purchases of the year in September (though year-to-date buying is about 7% below last year), and the World Gold Council noted gold ETF inflows have exceeded expectations, while it also cut its 2026 demand-growth forecast from 400 to 200 tonnes. [3][4] Commentators are split on the short term. Some quoted this week expect further weakness, while American Gold Exchange's Jim Wyckoff said a bottom "sooner rather than later" was likely "from a time perspective". [1] Several analysts cited the $3,400–$3,500 area as a zone the market is watching. [4] These are third-party opinions, reported for context, and we don't endorse any of them.

Crowd tone

Mostly negative. Headlines this week framed gold as a casualty of a hawkish Fed and a strong dollar, and the "meltdown" language was widespread. [1][4] Crowd tone describes what is being said. It isn't a forecast.

Questions worth asking yourself

  • If the December hike is already ~96% priced, what would count as a surprise in either direction?
  • Has gold been behaving like a safe haven this year, or like a source of cash when other assets fall? Standard Chartered's Suki Cooper argues it has been switching between the two roles. [3]
  • Do your own rules say anything about holding through a central-bank decision?
