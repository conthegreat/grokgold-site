2. AI can be wrong

The desk, the Telegram alerts and the outputs of the kit prompts use AI. Treat every AI passage as an untrusted draft of public information.

  • It can misread a release, invent a detail, or use a stale figure, and still sound sure.
  • A calendar heads-up can name the wrong time or the wrong previous print. Check the official release.
  • A daily digest can omit the fact that mattered.
  • A bull brief and a bear brief can disagree. That is two general readings of the same public material. It is not a signal, and it is not a forecast you should trade.
  • Your own AI tool can ignore the risk-rules in the kit. The kit does not supervise the tool.

Do not open, change or close a trade because an AI brief said something. If you want a recommendation that uses your circumstances, use an FCA-authorised adviser.

4. CFDs are high risk

If you trade gold CFDs, you can lose the money in that account quickly. Leverage magnifies losses. A provider can close positions when margin falls to its close-out level. Price can gap through a stop, so the loss can be larger than the stop distance. Spreads and overnight funding reduce the result. Do not trade with money you cannot afford to lose. A clear research note does not make a CFD safer.

Gold (XAU/USD) moves with US real yields, the dollar, inflation data, central-bank flows and geopolitics, including outside UK hours. Naming a release is not a view on how to trade it.

5. Protections that belong to a broker, not to us

Retail clients of FCA-authorised CFD providers generally receive leverage limits, close-out at 50% of required margin, and negative balance protection from that provider. Those duties are the provider’s. They may not apply to a professional account or to a firm outside the UK retail rules. Read the provider’s own percentage of retail accounts that lose money. We do not publish a percentage. We are not a provider, and nobody has given us a figure to use.
